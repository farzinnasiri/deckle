# Ghostwriter

Ghostwriter checks existing writing, then fixes only what the writer asks it to fix. Three metal toggle levers enable grammar, wording, and structure suggestions. Grammar and wording start enabled; structure starts disabled. Check always starts a fresh check on the current paper or selected passage. Its label and size stay the same while checking, reviewing, and fixing. The full current paper provides context for a selection. Nothing runs while you write unless you request a check.

Suggestions appear in a fixed metal proofreader beside the paper, with the original, replacement, and a short explanation on an inset slip. Its position and dimensions stay constant while navigating, applying corrections, or feeding the paper. Arrows move through the remaining suggestions and bring their ink into view. Fix one applies the visible correction immediately through the machine. Fix all applies every remaining suggestion. Navigating past a suggestion leaves it untouched. Phones reserve a lower tray and shorten the paper window, keeping both the writing and controls uncovered. The hardware options and proofreader take turns in that space; opening options puts the proofreader away, and a completed check returns to it. Longer suggestions scroll within the slip.

Closing the proofreader keeps the highlights; click a highlight to return, or Check to request a fresh pass. Reviews live only in memory for the current paper and account. Switching tabs, reloading the same desk snapshot, moving the caret, and changing formatting keep them. Whitespace changes anywhere are tolerated and rebase the offsets. Editing an affected word or correction region resolves that item and keeps the others, including while retyping it across several keystrokes. Changing other prose invalidates the pass. Switching papers or accounts, refreshing the page, or a successful fresh check replaces it. Undo and redo restore the matching suggestions from a bounded in-memory history. Reviews are temporary decorations, not saved ink, and do not add to writing activity.

The drawer emerges from underneath the machine. A slim dark end cap sits outside the right seam, with its mounting tucked into the case. It carries a ghost emblem, separator, the only visible Ghostwriter label, and a status lamp. Inside, three metal levers sit in recessed circular sockets with paired On/Off lamps. The labels and printed Check key follow the supplied hardware reference. Their motion, depth, and materials use CSS; no generated raster assets are needed. Desktop switches use a compact 76-pixel target and smaller sockets, labels, and a 100-by-44-pixel Check key before the shared scene scale. Bottom trays retain their original dimensions.

Check stays depressed with an amber edge light while reading or fixing, including backend retries. It stays labeled Check and is disabled until that operation finishes. Closing and reopening the drawer retains the request. Backgrounding the tab leaves inference running; changing unrelated prose rejects its late response. Escape cancels a check. Completion releases the key and leaves a green signal; failures light red and show an explanation. Press the same Check key to retry. Category switches remain usable between operations; their settings affect the next check.

On a full desktop, the side pull stays on the side. Its layout choice depends only on the viewport: at least 1100 pixels wide and 480 high, outside the phone layout. Notes, Settings, and camera scaling cannot switch it to a bottom tray or rearrange the machine's controls. A 174-pixel-high body adapts between 216 and 248 pixels wide and meets its 44-by-174-pixel pull. The closed end cap overlaps the case seam by four pixels and reserves no space on the machine's face. The resting desktop reserves enough room for the extended compact drawer. Wide desktops retain the 10% enlargement; constrained desktop viewports fit the machine and drawer together, before any opening. Opening or closing Ghostwriter leaves the machine, paper, lamp, and cord fixed. The drawer stays beyond the case wall without covering the controls or crossing the viewport edge. Narrower desktops and phones use a bottom tray. Notes and Ghostwriter are mutually exclusive in both directions, while an active check continues when Notes takes the drawer's place. A review that arrives during Notes or Settings stays put away without taking focus; its paper marks and pending suggestions remain available when Ghostwriter is reopened.

One CSS travel value keeps the drawer and pull attached through interrupted movement. The desktop drawer lives inside the machine and inherits its camera transform, so body and pull stay together without measuring their positions each frame. The close key, pull, and Escape dismiss the physical drawer. Ordinary desk controls leave it open. A modal temporarily puts it away and restores it afterward. The bottom tray uses a manual native popover, so unrelated clicks do not dismiss it. Keyboard activation and reduced motion skip the drawer slide. Mobile placement follows the visual viewport above the software keyboard. The proofreader and correction playback remain independent of drawer visibility. Desktop framing reserves space for the compact proofreader before it appears. Showing or closing the proofreader and opening the hardware drawer keep the scene fixed.

Opening and closing play edited CC0 recordings of a metal drawer through the existing volume and mute controls. Source and edits are documented in [audio credits](../public/audio/SOURCES.md).

During mechanical application, the paper feeds to each chosen passage before the machine erases and retypes the changed letters with the existing sounds and typebar. Identical ink stays in place. Changes run from the bottom of the paper upward so earlier positions remain correct. The strip shows Change 1/2 and so on. Each change's explanation is available through the strip's tooltip and live screen-reader status. This is an explicit editing pass, with no chat or generated continuation.

During application, Stop lives in the proofreader. Stop, Escape, clicking the paper, moving the carriage, or typing takes over. A partly edited passage is restored before human input continues. Completed passages remain and unapplied suggestions stay available. The editor's normal undo restores the last individual fix or Fix all group as one step; redo restores the corresponding review state. A handoff of unchanged paper preserves undo history. Reduced motion applies each local correction at once. Ghost edits retain the passage's ribbon and formatting and do not add to daily writing activity.

## Suggestion contract

The model returns strict JSON with an `edits` array. Each suggestion has an exact, nonempty `before` quotation, its `after` replacement, a `kind` of `grammar`, `wording`, or `structure`, and a short `reason`:

```json
{
  "edits": [
    {
      "before": "rain were",
      "after": "rain was",
      "kind": "grammar",
      "reason": "Match the singular subject."
    }
  ]
}
```

`before` must occur exactly once inside the requested editable range. Insertions quote enough existing context to locate the missing text; removals use an empty `after`. The model does not supply trusted character positions. `ghostEdits` in `lib/ghostwriter.ts` finds the exact quotation in the original snapshot and attaches UTF-16 `from` and `to` offsets. It rejects missing or ambiguous quotations, overlapping edits, changes outside the selected scope, protected clippings, and disabled categories. Both server and browser validate the plan. The paper highlights the changed ink within each quotation; an insertion marks the nearest original character without splitting a Unicode code point.

`lib/ghost-review.ts` tracks each suggestion's display quotation, minimal correction, and permitted manual-edit region. Reconciliation compares text content, maps whitespace changes, resolves local corrections, and rejects unrelated prose changes. The editor rechecks exact source offsets and current clipping provenance before applying. Playback commits each correction separately from its transient erasure/retyping frames, so interruption retains only completed edits. Applying suggestions is local and never requests another model response.

## Backend

Sign-in and an operator-assigned Ghostwriter feature flag are required for inference. Unassigned accounts do not see the controls and cannot start a check. See [feature flags](feature-flags.md) to enable or revoke a tester. Local writing and cloud saving remain independent. The authenticated Next.js route at `POST /api/ghostwriter` checks the Clerk account and origin, then uses Deckle's existing secret-protected Convex bridge to invoke an internal action. No provider credential reaches the browser. Convex mints a short-lived service token with `getServiceToken("ai-gateway")`. No new SDK is needed.

The model receives the current paper, editable range, protected clipping ranges, and enabled categories. It returns suggestions under the contract above. A response to an outdated paper is discarded. Review and application happen locally and do not require another inference call.

Only the current paper is sent when Check is pressed. Notes and the archive are not sent. Prompts and model responses are not saved separately or logged by this integration. Applied changes become ordinary local editing/replay events with `origin: "ghost"`, and follow the desk's existing cloud-save choice. The gateway request requires zero-data-retention endpoints and providers that do not collect data; if none are available, the request fails. Provider usage and billing metadata can still be retained.

Each pass reviews the complete editable scope, up to 12,000 UTF-16 code units, and returns all needed corrections without a suggestion-count cap. Inference makes at most three attempts, each with a 90-second timeout and a 16,000-token output allowance including reasoning. Truncated responses are retried rather than exposed as a partial review. Provider errors, network/timeouts, and unusable generated output retry after one second, then two seconds. Only the third inference failure is shown to the user, with sanitized diagnostics. Input, account, authentication, and local admission failures keep their immediate errors. The pass reserves and counts once across all attempts. The bridge allows 285 seconds, the browser 290 seconds, and the route 300 seconds, covering 273 seconds of inference and backoff. Per-account admission allows one active request, at least ten seconds between starts, and 30 starts per UTC day. Failed requests count toward the allowance because providers may charge for them. Stopping discards an outstanding response; a Convex action already running can finish its remaining attempts and still incur a charge. Reservations expire after five minutes if an action fails before cleanup.

## Model choice, checked 16 October 2026

The first model is `z-ai/glm-5.3-flash`, at `reasoning.effort: "low"`. Z.ai's latest Flash model requires reasoning and supports low, high, and max. Disabling thinking is rejected. Low is appropriate for a short copyediting pass. The prompt asks for minimal changes and preserves language, dialect, contractions, intentional fragments, facts, and meaning.

OpenRouter currently lists common GLM 5.3 Flash endpoints at $0.15 input and $0.50 output per million tokens, with observed provider throughput of roughly 86 to 187 tokens per second across several standard-price endpoints. These are provider observations, not Deckle measurements. Convex chooses the serving provider and does not accept throughput-based routing preferences. Requests cap acceptable provider prices at $0.50 input and $1.00 output per million tokens to prevent an unexpectedly expensive route. No frontier fallback is configured.

Claude Haiku 5.5 is the strongest next candidate to compare, at listed standard endpoint rates of $0.10 input and $0.50 output and 115 to 185 tokens per second. Kimi K2.6's listed headline output price is $3.20 per million tokens. Gemini 3.8 Flash lists $3.75 output at its current discount and roughly 81 to 106 tokens per second. Those two cost more for this small task. No public speed or coding benchmark establishes which best preserves a writer's voice. A comparison on representative English, Persian, and other user writing is still needed before claiming a quality winner.
