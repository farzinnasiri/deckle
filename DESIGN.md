# Deckle desk

The original direction comes from DECKLE.md sections 5 and 11. Farzin's updated reference makes the paper the protagonist: a large warm sheet, a slim aluminum foundation, a smaller manual, and a functional lamp pull. Finished pieces live in a paper tray beneath the machine. A quiet Focus control enters immersive writing.

Walnut, warm aluminum, ivory bakelite, charcoal ink, and a sparing vermilion accent. Subdued wood grain, shared lighting direction, restrained metal shading, and an irregular paper edge. Courier Prime gives the writing stronger contrast and a generous line rhythm; a restrained grotesque labels controls, and book serif distinguishes the manual and clippings. Mechanical controls have short travel, firm stops, clear purposes, and accessible names. Screen width and type preferences can change the editor's wrapping; archive previews reuse the saved marks and wrap to the available paper width.

The editor uses CodeMirror for native selection, keyboard navigation, composition, and wrapping. Edits are synchronous. Paper feed animates the entire paper layer, including its texture and header, in measured line increments. Caret navigation keeps the selected writing in view while manual feeding permits reading without moving the caret. Sound and motion surround the writing without delaying it. Immersive mode keeps the sheet and print rail in place while the body and its shadows fade together. A vertical control strip remains available. Reduced motion removes transitions.

Build direction is inherited from the supplied brief. Materials are implemented as textured CSS layers in this browser version; they are not Blender PBR assets. Light adjusts the whole scene. Motion respects the system and manual reduced-motion settings.

## Second pass: the typing experience

The paper moves, never the line. Writing happens on a fixed print line just above the slot, the head sits under the next cell, and the paper climbs as lines are added. This is DECKLE.md §6.1–6.2 taken literally, and it is what makes each Return feel like a mechanism.

Chrome is removed rather than restyled: no header, date line, hints, captions or persistent save note. The date is a rubber stamp on the paper; the lamp is only its cord; focus mode is a quiet icon. Labels on the machine are engraved Barlow, not UI text.

Motion rules: escapement steps are ~34 ms, the carriage return ~290 ms with a hair of settle, line feeds 150 ms with a small overshoot, toggles snap past centre. Light changes over 700 ms with a filament flicker. Everything respects reduced motion.

## Writing-first refinement

The continuous roll feeds directly into the machine slot, with the typing line fixed just above it. Raised formatting keys and a bichrome ribbon rocker sit on the metal faceplate alongside the feed knob and Tear key. Controls use their actual layout size on phones, with at least 44px touch targets. The raised Settings tab pulls out a layered card while the desk moves into its preview position. Closing returns both along the same path. Reduced motion uses a short fade. Settings opens a narrow inspector beside the actual desk. Its overview leads to customization, sound, and author. Customization exposes model, finish, paper, lighting, and surface controls while hiding the archive and desk chrome. Every choice changes the live scene immediately. On phones, the inspector sits below the visible machine with its own scrolling controls. The desk stays fixed to the viewport; scrolling over the sheet feeds the continuous roll, and settings and archived writing scroll inside their own panels. Machine keys share each model's materials and sit in a recessed key bed. Model and finish are the only machine appearance controls. Desk presets live under Desk and explain their combined paper, surface, and lighting changes. Paper uses a fine scanned-style cotton grain, tinted by the chosen stock, with restrained fiber contrast. The owner card and Draft/Ink switch are removed. The clock is optional and moves within the desk bounds. Print uses a separate document with 12pt type, A4 margins, and full author and date attribution.

## Desk objects and paper height

The writing machine is wider and sits lower, exposing more of the continuous paper roll above the print line. The tray keeps a clear gap below it. The optional clock moves across the visible desk rather than staying inside the centered instrument area. Clock faces stay upright; analog models show a seconds hand, with the marine model using its seconds subdial. Settings uses a gear with “Make it yours”; Focus uses a focus icon with “Just the page”, changing to “Back to the desk” in immersive mode.

The wastebasket appears only inside the archive and uses six photographic PNG states, empty through five discarded papers. A full basket blocks further discards and explains how to empty it. Emptying retains the permanent-deletion confirmation, then feeds each paper through a photographic shredder in sequence. Each paper is removed only after reaching the cutters; leaving the view stops the remaining queue. Reduced motion removes the feed animation. Generated object sources and prompts live in public/objects/SOURCES.md.

## Focus and everyday controls

Settings, Focus, global mute, and quick paper settings form a vertical strip at the top right. Desktop controls retain their side labels; phones use labeled 44px icon buttons. The quick paper card opens beside the strip, or below it on phones, without disabling writing. It offers the installed fonts, Standard/Larger type, and paper ruling. Clicking outside or pressing Escape dismisses it.

Focus keeps the paper and rail geometry stable. The machine body and both body shadows fade together over 200ms, with only a short downward movement. The rail keeps its own shallow contact shadow. Reduced motion changes state immediately.

Global mute silences the master audio bus, including ongoing voices and room reflections, while preserving volume and ticking preferences. The volume light goes dark, the wheel rests at zero, and the clock crown moves to off. Unmuting restores those preferences. Moving the physical volume wheel or turning ticking on deliberately restores sound. The Sound inspector shares the same mute state.

## Multiple open tabs

Tabs share one local desk. Writing ownership follows focus or interaction, and changes broadcast to passive tabs after saving. A handoff flushes pending writes before the next tab loads the desk. Input and button clicks during the brief handoff wait for the editor to be ready. A tear already moving into the archive finishes before ownership changes; an abandoned manual tear lets go. Background tabs keep their complete scene and do not tick audibly. The permanent second-tab warning is removed. Local undo history clears when another tab supplies a snapshot, preventing an old undo from replacing writing made elsewhere.

The capsule and its permanent typebar share a measured print origin. A short contact and return happens at the inserted character before the capsule advances; reduced motion retains the mechanism without the tap. Manual carriage travel plays short, velocity-weighted rail sounds through the same muted audio bus as typing.

Lighting has two independent sources: a photographic pendant above the desk and an upward wash from a strip integrated into the print rail. The cord switches the pendant; each machine has a recessed vertical LIGHT fader controlling the rail. Customize desk → Light selects which lamp to adjust, with separate brightness, colour, spread, and reach. Preset bulb tones and custom colours affect the emitter, illumination, and highlights. Both lamps can be on at once. The rail source follows the actual machine position in responsive layouts, and paper and mechanism shadows follow the balance between the two sources. A shared exposure field covers the scene, so switching off the room and both lamps leaves the desk, paper, machine, clock, and tray near black. Controls remain findable. Both lamps feed a shared low-resolution diffuse field and a separate specular field; the original material textures remain at full resolution. The overhead source uses a finite aperture, distance falloff and approximate surface normals for the paper, curved deck, machine face and wood. This is a calibrated shallow-scene approximation, not a ray-traced scene. The rail uses a finite horizontal strip with short-range transmission at the slot and longer-range scattering through the sheet. A recessed luminous diffuser establishes the source. Spill reaches the desk around the housing ends, with a widening soft shadow in front of the machine. Surface maps follow measured responsive geometry and the exposed sheet as it feeds. Material details and contact shadows stay in the existing CSS. Light fields only recalculate when geometry or lamp settings change.

## Archive drawer

Save to archive detaches the current paper, then carries an intact copy from the exposed writing area into the measured tray slot. The sheet flattens as it settles into the stack; a fresh roll follows. The browser animation API handles the flight and cancellation without a canvas renderer. Reduced motion files immediately. The current paper still saves locally while typing.

The tray opens a wooden drawer rendered in Three.js. Upright teal folders recede into depth, with cream title tabs physically attached to each cover. There are no navigation buttons below the drawer. The wheel, sideways dragging and swiping browse the folders; clicking a title tab selects it. Clicking the selected folder or pulling it upwards reads its paper directly. A deliberate pull of at least 48px opens the preview; shorter pulls and cancelled gestures settle back over 220ms. Gesture direction locks after the initial movement so browsing cannot turn into an accidental opening. Hover only warms the tab and changes the cursor. Folders already browsed lean towards the front lip and remain visible, with their tabs available to navigate back. Reversing direction stands them upright again. Scroll and drag follow the input directly, then settle on a folder; clicking a tab retargets a 220ms movement from the current pose. Keyboard arrows and Home/End browse, Enter/Space reads, and Escape returns from reading before closing the archive, with immediate keyboard transitions. Tab hit areas project the actual 3D corners and layer in physical depth order. Clicking the exposed drawer beside the preview returns the paper through the reverse transition; the toolbar also retains its return icon. The drawer stays mounted behind the reading view, preserving its camera, selection and textures. Open brings the paper into the typewriter and focuses the writer after hydrating its saved event log; current writing is filed first. Search filters the drawer without opening a paper. One top menu holds export, print, replay, backup and import. Discard shares that toolbar while reading. The primary paper action is Open, or Restore in the wastebasket, and replay controls appear only during replay.

The 3D drawer uses the existing wood and paper textures, rough cardboard, warm directional light and soft occlusion shadows. Rendering runs only during navigation, pulling, resizing or texture loading. Pixel ratio is capped, and geometry and label textures are limited to the nearby folders. Closing the drawer disposes its renderer and resources; Three.js is loaded only when needed. Reduced motion snaps navigation into place and keeps the pull gesture without moving the folder. If WebGL is unavailable, accessible folder buttons still reach the preview.

The home desk has no wastebasket. A larger photographic basket stands beside the drawer and opens the discarded-paper view, which uses a creased sheet and a row of thumbnails. Restore returns the paper to its open folder. Emptying requires a compact confirmation with Cancel focused first, then retains the sequential shredder animation. Closing stops remaining shredding. On phones the camera pulls back to keep the actual drawer in view; swipe navigation uses the same physical stack. The basket sits beside its base. Paper content scrolls independently, toolbar controls remain at least 44px, and reduced motion removes folder lifts and tosses while preserving feedback.

## Light that behaves like light

The pendant lays an elliptical pool on the desk, centred below its shade: cos³ falloff from a hanging point source, cut off softly at the bowl's rim angle. The paper sits inside that pool, so the wood around it keeps its grain under the lamp, and the machine casts a short, soft shadow towards the reader. The sheet itself falls off towards its corners instead of clipping flat. Looking up into the lit shade shows an overexposed bulb, an enamel bowl that darkens towards its grazing sides, a brass lip lit from inside, and a bloom that spills over the rim; the bloom reads strongly only in a dark room.

The rail strip sits just in front of the sheet, so its light arrives at a grazing angle and falls off steeply up the paper. Near the slot that grazing light picks out the fibres: `paper-cotton-raking.png`, generated from the cotton scan by `scripts/paper-relief.mjs`, shares the paper's tile and travels with it.

## Motion and sound pass (Oct 2026)

The rules live in `app/motion.css`, loaded after `globals.css`, with new voices in `lib/audio.ts`. Every moving part has a reduced-motion branch that fades instead.

**Battery.** Clicking it releases a brass plate with a relay click; the plate is drawn out of the housing (400ms, a hair of settle) and lands on a stop. The five cells run a lamp test up to today's charge, then the counter drums tumble to their figures, each with its own tick (`meterOpen`, `meterCharge`, `counterRoll`). The plate states the charge rules itself, so the hover tooltip only appears while it is closed, after a hover has rested for half a second. Closing slides the plate home, quicker and quieter.

**Settings inspector.** The sheet travels in from the desk's edge (440ms, a slight turn, a hair of overshoot) and is put away in 260ms. The things it covers (wall switch, clock, cord, battery) fade and drift instead of vanishing. Its contents are set down after the sheet. Moving between pages slides horizontally in the direction of travel. Focus lands on the sheet, not its first button, so a mouse-opened panel has no keyboard ring.

**Archive.** The tray is drawn toward you when it opens. Reading a paper draws the sheet up out of its folder from behind the folder's front, where Put back and Open sit; the drawer behind steps away entirely rather than ghosting. An empty archive (or a search with no matches) is still the drawer, with a note lying in it. The wooden-drawer recording, folder friction and paper pulls are separate voices for opening, closing, lifting and returning.

**Guide cue.** When the setup guide or the Light page points at the lamp or the rail fader, the object breathes twice. It no longer draws an outline around the lamp button.

Build note: Turbopack's CSS transform drops `translate` and `rotate` declared beside a `transform` in the same rule. Use one `transform` there.

## Scissors, tape and the copy holder (Oct 2026)

**Cut, copy, paste.** Edits stay synchronous; the paper objects are drawn around them (`components/scissors.ts`, `lib/clipboard.ts`). Cutting snips the selection with two scissor closes: a dashed edge traces it, the strip lifts off the sheet and rides near the pointer. Pasting your own words tapes the strip down where they land; typing anything else puts it aside. Copying is a faint carbon slip.

Provenance travels with the text in a private clipboard type, with a remembered-copy fallback for browsers that drop it. The rules:
- Your own writing, copied from this paper or one in the archive, comes back as your own typing. Its clippings come back as clippings.
- Text from outside is foreign material. Passages are taped in as clippings; a short single-line fragment (48 characters or fewer, such as a name or a URL) is typed in, with the machine running through it.
- Research copied from the copy holder is always a clipping unless you ask otherwise.
- ⌘⇧V types any outside text in as yours.
- After an outside paste, a small tag on the pasted words says what happened and swaps it to the other form. The swap keeps a single undo step.
- Moved and retyped text does not count toward today's characters.
- A paste too long for the paper goes on the copy holder instead.
- Dropping text from outside follows the same rules. Images are not accepted anywhere.

**Copy holder.** One sheet of notes per paper, held by a clip in a drawer at the desk's left edge. Only its leather pull tab shows. Drag the tab (it follows the hand, resists past its ends, and a flick decides) or click it; the sheet slides out and latches with a click, and the desk makes room on the same curve (while the tab is dragged, the desk follows the hand). A release the page never hears, such as letting go outside the window, settles the drawer from where the pointer is. Write or paste anything on it; a pencil scratch accompanies typing. Copied or dragged onto the paper, its words arrive as clippings. Notes are stored with the piece (`Piece.notes`), go into the archive with it, and come back when it is reopened; sheets from the first version are read into the notes. The sheet's stock is chosen under Customize desk → Paper and kept in this browser, because desk settings are validated by the cloud backend. Focus mode puts the drawer away.

**The camera's moves.** Opening Settings or the copy holder moves the whole desk aside on one drawer curve (440ms, the Settings panel's own timing), both ways. The move starts at the click: the new transforms are written before React renders, and React's state follows a frame later, so a heavy render (Settings mounts a whole panel) happens while the compositor is already easing the desk. During the move the desk sits on its own layer. Closing Settings over an open copy holder goes straight to the holder's view.

**A strip in hand.** Cutting snips the words out and copying peels a duplicate off them (no blades; the words stay). Either way the strip rides just below the pointer's tip until it is pasted, where it is taped down. Click where it should go and rest a moment: a "Paste here" key appears at the caret, pasting the strip without asking the browser for the clipboard. Typing or Escape puts the strip aside; so does a long wait.

**Selection bar.** A settled selection on the paper gets a short bar of keys: Cut, Copy, Paste, then Bold, Italic, Underline, Strike through and Code. It sits above the words, or below when the paper window has no room above, close to where the mouse let go, and never appears mid-drag. Keys show their name and shortcut after the pointer rests (⌘B, ⌘I, ⌘U, ⌘⇧X, ⌘E) and stay pressed when the whole selection has that style. Code is a slate ribbon over a faint grey ground and exports as backticks; strike through is the machine's x-out and exports as `~~`. The paste tag shares the bar's look.

**Moving around the paper.** A click on an earlier line moves the caret, not the paper; this now holds even when the page re-renders in the same frame (measure requests keep the strongest reason: edit, then click, then keyboard, then layout). Keyboard movement and selection leave the paper still while the caret is in the window, feeding only enough to keep it in view. So do commands on words already on the page: styles, cut, paste, drop, undo and redo (the words stay under the hand). Only a keystroke rolls the line back to the print line first, at a pace that grows with the distance. Each way of turning the platen has its own pace (`data-roll` on the strip): wheel input is gathered into one move per frame and chased with a short ease-out, the knob follows the hand, and the light on the sheet always shares the strip's timing.

**Right-to-left.** A line runs in the direction of its first letter, and empty lines inherit from above. Persian and Arabic are set in Vazirmatn and Hebrew in Noto Sans Hebrew (OFL, `public/fonts`). Both are registered under the existing family names with `unicode-range`, so the page, clippings, title and archive labels all pick them up and only load them when needed. Arabic-script letters skip the per-glyph strike offsets so their joins hold. The carriage and type guide follow the caret in either direction.

**Title.** The title is typed on the same machine: keys strike, and new letters land with wet ink. The input keeps native editing above an inked copy of its glyphs (`components/paper-title.tsx`).
